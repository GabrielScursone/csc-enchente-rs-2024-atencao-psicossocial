    About the study

    Ecological time-series study of the 497 municipalities of Rio Grande do Sul. The question is whether the flood of April and May 2024 changed psychiatric admissions in the SUS and care in CAPS, and whether the change was larger where a larger share of the population was affected.

    Data

    • Admissions: reduced hospital admission records (AIH) of the SIH/SUS (RD files), type 1 records of state residents, dated by the date of admission. Psychiatric admission: principal diagnosis in chapter F of the ICD-10 (F00–F99).
    • CAPS: psychosocial RAAS records of the SIA/SUS (PS files), by municipality of residence and month of care. Users seen: user-months, the same measure used by TabNet.
    • Exposure: population living in the area hit by floods and landslides (area mapped by INPE and ANA, version 3), calculated by IBGE with the addresses of the 2022 Census.
    • Population: Ministry of Health estimates by municipality, interpolated month by month.

    Analyses

    • Observed × expected: the number expected without the flood comes from the average of three models (quasi-Poisson regression, SARIMA and LightGBM) trained up to April 2024 and tested on four fictitious dates. An O/E below 1 indicates less care than expected.
    • Dose-response: Poisson regression with municipality and month fixed effects. The rate ratio (RR) compares municipalities that differ by 10 percentage points in the share of the population affected. The event study shows the RR quarter by quarter; coefficients far from 1 before the flood indicate a pre-trend.
    • Robustness: exclusion of Porto Alegre and of the Taquari Valley (also hit in 2023), region × year fixed effects, a fictitious flood date, synthetic difference-in-differences (SDID) and analysis of CAPS by establishment.
    • Periods: acute (May–Jul 2024), subacute (Aug 2024–Apr 2025) and late (May 2025–Mar 2026).

    How to read

    The counts are care funded by the SUS, not people or the prevalence of mental disorders. A drop may reflect a disruption of access or of records, not less distress. For this reason, childbirth, appendicitis and all other admissions serve as comparison outcomes. The data are public and aggregated, with no individual identification.
